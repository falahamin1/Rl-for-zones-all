# Results summary — modesel, gridtimed, trmgrid

All 288 configs in `run_one.TARGET_RUNS` are complete: every `results/*__manifest.json`
shows `total_env_steps >= 300000` or a set `early_stop_step` (checked directly
against the manifests, not against `results/ckpt/*.pt` — this machine's local
torch/numpy (`numpy 1.24.3`) can't unpickle the checkpoints Alpine wrote
(`No module named 'numpy._core'`, a numpy<2 vs >=2 mismatch); that only
affects loading `.pt` files locally, not the jsonl logs this report is built
from, and it doesn't reflect anything wrong with the runs themselves).

- **modesel**: 2 instances (M3, M4) × 6 encoders × 3 seeds = 36 runs
- **gridtimed**: 2 instances (T2, T3) × 6 encoders × 10 seeds = 120 runs (plus
  a separate 12-run shaping experiment on T2/mlp_point+deepset_h, not covered
  here — see `artifacts/PART1_FINAL.md`)
- **trmgrid**: 2 instances (G_SMALL, G_LARGE) × 6 encoders × 10 seeds = 120 runs

Numbers below come straight from `analysis/summary.txt` / `analysis/steps_to_threshold.csv`
/ `analysis/encoder_stats.csv`, all generated by `python3 analyze.py`, which
now covers all three domains natively — `trmgrid` is in `DOMAINS_INSTANCES`
alongside `modesel`/`gridtimed` (previously it was analyzed via a one-off
script reusing this module's functions; that's been folded in properly, see
`artifacts/RESULTS_CHECK.md` §4). `analysis/*.png` curves exist for all three
domains, including `trmgrid__G_SMALL__curves_{steps,wallclock}.png` and
`trmgrid__G_LARGE__curves_{steps,wallclock}.png`, produced by the same
`analyze.py` run as everything else.

**Metrics**: `final_ep_return_mean` is the last logged training-episode return
(mean±std across seeds — sign/scale is domain-specific, higher is better in
all three). `overall`/`last200` success rate come from each run's
`__episodes.jsonl` (true per-episode outcome, all training episodes vs. just
the last 200). `greedy`/`stochastic success rate` and `greedy cycling frac`
are gridtimed/trmgrid-only: a periodic deterministic + stochastic policy
rollout against a fixed eval seed (`gridtimed_eval.py` / `trmgrid_eval.py`),
last valid value before training ended — not applicable to modesel.
`steps_to_50/75/90pct` is env_steps to close that fraction of each run's own
[worst, best] smoothed-return range (sample efficiency, independent of
absolute return scale).

**Reading it**: trmgrid is clearly the hardest of the three benchmarks —
best last-200 success rate tops out around 0.73–0.74 (deepset_h / gnn on
G_SMALL, cnn_dbm / cnn_proj on G_LARGE) vs. 0.90–1.00 for the best encoders
on gridtimed. `deepset_h` and `gnn` are the strongest pair here, consistent
with them (plus cnn_dbm/cnn_proj) being the top group on gridtimed too.
`mlp_point` is clearly the weakest surviving encoder (never breaks a 0.31
success rate, 0 greedy success both instances) — plausible given trmgrid's
branching zones need real geometric reasoning that a flat point encoding
doesn't provide. `deepset_v` is the standout failure: essentially 0% success
on both instances, worse than its bimodal-but-sometimes-working behavior on
gridtimed. **Checked, not just a scope limitation**: `vrep_fallback_frac`
(the fraction of `deepset_v` forward passes that hit
`zoneviews.zone.VertexEnumerationLimit` and fall back to bounding-box
corners instead of real vertices) is exactly **0.0 across every one of the
120 trmgrid runs and every one of the 120 gridtimed runs** — the fallback
path never fires on either domain at these clock counts. A direct sample of
~450 zones per instance (random-action rollouts) confirms why: affine
dimension stays in the 0–4 range on both trmgrid instances, nowhere near
where `C(m', affine_dim)` could threaten the 200,000-subset exact-enumeration
limit. trmgrid's zones aren't structurally higher-dimensional than
gridtimed's either (G_LARGE's sampled zones skew *lower*-dimensional than
T2/T3's). So this is a genuine finding about the V-rep representation
itself failing to learn trmgrid's branching structure, not an artifact of
hitting a vertex-enumeration scope limit — full derivation in
`artifacts/RESULTS_CHECK.md`.

## Cross-benchmark takeaways

- **`mlp_point`'s weakness is domain-specific, not universal.** On
  gridtimed and trmgrid it's a clear, consistent failure by every measure
  (return, success rate, greedy vs. stochastic all agree it's near 0%). On
  **modesel it's competitive on success rate** (essentially tied with the
  top two on M3, mid-pack and ahead of three other encoders on M4) even
  though its final-return magnitude looks worse — those are different axes
  there, and only one of them (success rate) is the fair "is it competent"
  question. The zone-aware encoders' advantage over `mlp_point` is real on
  gridtimed/trmgrid specifically, not a blanket property of point vs. zone
  representations.
- **`cnn_proj` and `cnn_dbm` (grid-rasterization encoders) are the most
  reliable performers overall** — best or near-best on modesel, clearly best
  on gridtimed (with the tightest variance of any encoder by far), and
  in the top tier on trmgrid.
- **`deepset_h` and `gnn` are the strongest pair specifically on the two
  branching/timing domains (gridtimed, trmgrid)**, not modesel — suggests
  their relative-set/graph structure helps most when the environment has
  real topological branching to reason about.
- **`deepset_v` (v-rep) is the one encoder whose behavior doesn't
  generalize across domains**: fine on modesel, high-variance/bimodal on
  gridtimed, and a near-complete, low-variance failure on trmgrid (std
  tightens to ±0.36 on G_LARGE — it's reliably failing, not randomly
  struggling). Confirmed this is a real representation-learning failure,
  not a vertex-enumeration scope artifact: `vrep_fallback_frac` is exactly
  0.0 on every trmgrid *and* gridtimed run, and trmgrid's zones aren't
  higher-dimensional than gridtimed's (affine dim 0–4 on both, sampled
  directly). Still an open question *why* v-rep specifically fails to
  learn trmgrid's branching structure, but it's not the enumeration limit.
- **trmgrid is a harder benchmark than gridtimed** by every encoder's
  success-rate ceiling, which is presumably the point of having added it.
